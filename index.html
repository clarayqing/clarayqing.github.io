Research project2026

Beyond the
ideal-gas law.

Physics-aware symbolic correction for CO₂

I use interpretable equations to model the compressibility correction, ΔZ. The workflow combines CoolProp reference data, regime-specific symbolic regression with PySR, and a soft gate that blends the predictions.

Domain & evaluation notes +

The natural grid covers 310–500 K and molar densities of 100–20,000 mol/m³. Complete temperature isotherms are held out for validation and testing.

The high-density stress grid is inside that domain; it tests a distribution shift, not out-of-range extrapolation. Maximum pointwise pressure errors are 9.064% on the natural test and 5.186% on the stress test.

04 How I think

Small steps.
Hard problems.

Whether I’m writing C++ or testing a physical model, I tend to return to the same three habits.

01 ↗

Find the structure.

Start with examples. Look for an invariant or a simpler version of the problem. Choose a representation that makes the useful details visible.

02 ∴

Make it precise.

Explain why the algorithm works. State the assumptions behind the model. Test the edge cases that a good-looking result might hide.

03 ↻

Keep asking.

A wrong answer is useful information. Work out which assumption failed, write down what changed, and try the next, more specific question.

There’s always another good question.
